95 characters.
Zero extras.

Letters, numbers, punctuation, and spaces. The 95 printable ASCII characters are the entire vocabulary.

01 / THE MESSAGE

Words stay words.

No photos. No videos. No attachments. No emoji, hidden characters, or rich formatting. Links stay plain text.

02 / THE ENCRYPTION

For your contact.

Messages are encrypted on your device using Signal’s official libsignal. The relay delivers ciphertext; it does not hold the keys to read your text.

03 / THE BOUNDARY

Checked on arrival.

The receiving app validates decrypted bytes before displaying anything. Non-ASCII and non-printable content is rejected, with an alert.

Small by design

A conversation.
Nothing attached.

ISYEET has one job: move encrypted text between people. That is the product, and the limit.

A familiar address.
Sign in with Google and add contacts by Gmail or Google Workspace address. Independently compare identity fingerprints through a trusted channel before messaging.
Seven-day messages.
Read or unread, text has a fixed deadline seven days after sending. Retries never restart the clock. Suspended apps clean up expired history before showing it again.
Direct text. Small groups.
Message one person, test delivery to yourself, or talk in a fixed group of up to eight people. Each recipient gets a separate end-to-end encrypted copy. Titles and membership stay fixed. Every member independently verifies the others.
The same size on the wire.
Messages of up to 1,024 characters share the same 8,192-byte encrypted envelope. Direct text, group copies and cover packets all use that size.
Optional cover traffic.
Choose generic “New message” notifications or quiet cover mode; only one can be on. While the app is active, cover mode offers a sending slot every 15–45 seconds: queued text first, otherwise an encrypted random nonce for an eligible contact. Both verified contacts must opt in and exchange real messages. It pauses in the background. Cover packets never appear in your inbox. Budgets and peer availability can pause them too; this reduces some traffic clues but does not prevent traffic analysis.
A smaller surface.
No media decoders, link previews, or message formatting engine. Fewer things to parse, and fewer ways for a message to pretend to be something else.
